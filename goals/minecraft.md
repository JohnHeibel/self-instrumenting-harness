# Goal: build a base, collect resources, craft widely, mine diamonds, and try to beat the Ender Dragon in Minecraft

The program is **Minecraft: Java Edition 1.21.1** with NeoForge and the Minescript mod, in a **survival, normal-difficulty singleplayer world** (name `sisyphus-mc1`, fixed seed `sisyphus1`, cheats off) that is already open. You are the only player. You start with nothing in your inventory.

**Objective, in priority order:**
1. **Build a base and accumulate resources.** Wood, stone and coal first, then a shelter you can sleep in, a furnace, chests, a farm or other steady food, and iron. Keep the base stocked; resources are what the later steps spend.
2. **Craft as many distinct things as you can.** Breadth counts: the number of different items you have crafted is measured. Tools, armor, utility blocks, redstone items, food, brewing and enchanting gear, and so on.
3. **Diamonds**, then an enchanting table, then the Nether (blaze rods), eyes of ender, the stronghold and the End.
4. **Try to beat the Ender Dragon.** Death is part of the game (you respawn); retrieve your things or restock from the base.

**Progress** is measured by these steps, judged from the world's own files (advancements and statistics, saved about every five minutes): first wood, crafting table, stone tools, furnace, storage chest placed, shelter built (many building blocks placed and a door or bed), iron ingot, iron tools, crops planted, animals bred, 10 and 25 distinct items crafted, diamonds, enchanting table, 50 distinct items crafted, Nether entered, blaze rod, first eye of ender, 12 eyes, stronghold found, 75 distinct items crafted, End entered, dragon killed. **Tokens** and wall-clock time are everything you spend. The point of this run is to build tools and hooks that take each step in fewer calls and less time.

**Build reusable tools early.** Most of the cost is seeing and repeating. Write Minescript scripts (see the host notes) that read the world and act: state readers (position, inventory, hotbar, open screen and container, nearby blocks and entities, health and hunger), walking and mining routines, craft-and-smelt sequences, a "find the nearest tree, ore or animal" scan, and loops that chop, mine or farm many times in one call. Prefer one script that does a whole task (gather a stack of logs, craft a pickaxe, dig down to a level) over many single actions and screenshots. Keep a bridge script serving your state over HTTP so your tools can read it between actions, and save repeated work with `run_script`.

**Player actions only.** Hooks and scripts may read anything the client knows. What they do to the world must be what a player can do: walk and jump, look around, break blocks you can reach with the right tool and the normal mining time, place blocks and items from your inventory, use blocks, open containers and move items between slots, craft in the 2x2 or crafting-table grid, attack and eat, sleep. The game is the real client at normal speed (a script may press keys and click as a player would, at about 4 actions per second for discrete actions).

Not allowed, and flagged or voiding the run: chat or console commands that change the game (`/give`, `/tp`, `/gamemode`, `/time`, `/setblock`, `/summon`, and the like), turning on cheats or opening the world to LAN, editing the world, save or game files, spawning or duplicating items or entities, teleporting, instant mining or crafting, reaching beyond a player's reach, and changing the game mode or difficulty. Minescript's own commands (`\jobs`, `\killjob`, your scripts) are fine. A grader reads the world files and logs for cheats, the game mode and commands used; your extensions' code and every call to them are recorded and reviewed for the rest.

**Long jobs.** The bridge can run a job in the background while your sandbox waits for it. Make the wait one call that polls every second until the job ends or about 4 minutes pass (not 25 seconds), and have the bridge finish with a short result. Keep per-request HTTP timeouts short, but wait in a loop.

**Efficiency hints.** Craft torches (coal or charcoal plus a stick) before entering a cave: a dark cave is slow and dangerous to scan. For iron, a branch tunnel at about y=16 (or exposed ore on hillsides and ravines) usually finds it faster than wandering caves; scan with your block reader for `iron_ore` and `coal_ore` in a radius before walking. Carry water or blocks to cover lava. Smelt in batches (a furnace takes about 10 s per item), and craft everything you can in one table visit. Eat before you are hungry, and sleep through the first night in a bed or a sealed shelter.
